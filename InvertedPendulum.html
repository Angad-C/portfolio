Disturbance Rejection System

In this project, we modeled Rocky as a balancing system and used experimental calibration data to design closed-loop controllers for disturbance rejection[cite: 12]. We first estimated the motor constants, natural frequency, and effective pendulum length from measured data[cite: 12].

These parameters were then used to build transfer function models and design both 3-pole and 5-pole control systems in MATLAB and Simulink[cite: 12].

Parameter Estimation

We leveraged load_motor_data() and fitted an exponential curve to the data of both motors ramping up[cite: 12].

Motor & Pendulum Characterization

  • Motor Constants

    The motor ramp-up fit curve equation used was[cite: 12]:

    \[ v(t)=c(1-e^{-\alpha t}) \]

    We estimated final values of $c_L=0.6983$, $\tau_L=0.0461$, $\alpha_L=21.7028$, and a steady-state gain of $\beta_L=0.0023$ for the left motor[cite: 12]. The right motor yielded $c_R=0.6707$, $\tau_R=0.0436$, $\alpha_R=22.9444$, and $\beta_R=0.0022$[cite: 12].

  • Natural Frequency

    Positive peaks were detected from the shifted pendulum response to estimate the damped oscillation frequency[cite: 12]. Using the fitted data, the final values obtained were $\omega_n=4.3855$ rad/s and an effective pendulum length of $l_{eff}=0.5101$ m[cite: 12].

Closed-Loop Controllers

Designing both 3-pole and 5-pole systems for optimal disturbance rejection[cite: 12].

3-Pole Initial System

We set our damping ratio to be 0.9 and wanted Rocky to return to its original angle as quickly as possible with minimal oscillations[cite: 12]. Pole 3 needed to minimally affect the two dominant poles while fulfilling the constraint that the average of the real components of the three poles equalled $-\alpha/3$[cite: 12]. This yielded gains of $K_p=791.4680$ and $K_i=5336.6$[cite: 12].

5-Pole System Upgrade

We made 2 new poles to increase the stability of our system by combatting steady state error accumulation and increased positional control[cite: 12]. The 5 pole system adds a motor feedback controller $J(s)$ in the lower feedback path to improve the regulation of the Rocky velocity and position while maintaining stable angle control[cite: 12].

Simulated Behavior

Disturbance impulses are applied at the output to test how the system responds to disturbances[cite: 12].

3-Pole Runaway

For the 3-pole system, the Rocky has a relatively constant body angle, a constantly increasing cart velocity with a step behavior, and a rapidly increasing cart position[cite: 12]. Whenever we simulated a disturbance, the Rocky increased its velocity by ~32.5 m/s, but ended up running away from its initial position indefinitely[cite: 12].

5-Pole Stability

The 5-pole system allows the system to recover from the disturbances without the runaway position behavior seen in the 3-pole case[cite: 12]. The Rocky keeps the body angle, cart velocity, and cart position constant at 0[cite: 12].

Final Outcome

Whenever we simulated a disturbance on the 5-pole system, the Rocky was able to successfully rebalance and bring its velocity, position, and angle back to 0 at a maximum of ~6 seconds between them[cite: 12].
